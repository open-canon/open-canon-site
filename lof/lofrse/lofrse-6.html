Lectures on Faith (Revised Standard Edition) — Lecture Sixth

1 In the preceding lectures we treated of the ideas of the character, perfections, and attributes of God.
2 We now proceed to treat of the knowledge which persons must have, that the course of life they pursue is according to the will of God, in order that they may be enabled to exercise faith in him unto life and salvation.
3 This knowledge occupies an important place in revealed religion; for it was by means of it that the ancients were enabled to endure "as seeing him who is invisible."
4 An actual knowledge, to any person, that the course of life which he pursues is according to the will of God, is essentially necessary to enable him to have that confidence in God without which no person can obtain eternal life.
5 It was this knowledge that enabled the ancient saints to endure all their afflictions and persecutions, and to take joyfully the spoiling of their goods, knowing (not merely believing) that they had "a more enduring substance" (Hebrews 10:34).
6 Having the assurance that they were pursuing a course agreeable to the will of God, they were enabled not only to take the spoiling of their goods and the wasting of their substance joyfully, but also to suffer death in its most horrid forms, knowing (not merely believing) that "when this earthly house of their tabernacle was dissolved, they had a building of God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens" (2 Corinthians 5:1).
7 Such was, and always will be, the situation of the saints of God, that unless they have an actual knowledge that the course they are pursuing is according to the will of God, they will grow weary in their minds and faint.
8 For such has been, and always will be, the opposition in the hearts of unbelievers, and of those who know not God, against the pure and unadulterated religion of heaven (the only thing that ensures eternal life), that they will persecute to the uttermost all who worship God according to his revelations, receive the truth in the love of it, and submit to be guided and directed by his will.
9 They will drive them to such extremities that nothing short of an actual knowledge of their being the favorites of heaven, and of their having embraced that order of things which God has established for the redemption of man, will enable them to exercise that confidence in him necessary for them to overcome the world and obtain that crown of glory laid up for those who fear God.
10 For a man to lay down his all—his character and reputation, his honor and applause, his good name among men, his houses, his lands, his brothers and sisters, his wife and children, and even his own life also—counting all things but filth and dross for the excellency of the knowledge of Jesus Christ, requires more than mere belief or supposition that he is doing the will of God.
11 It requires actual knowledge, a realization that when these sufferings are ended he will enter into eternal rest and become a partaker of the glory of God.
12 For unless a person knows that he is walking according to the will of God, it would be offering an insult to the dignity of the Creator for him to say that he would be a partaker of God's glory when he is done with the things of this life.
13 But when he has this knowledge, and most assuredly knows that he is doing the will of God, his confidence can be equally strong that he will be a partaker of the glory of God.
14 Let us here observe that a religion which does not require the sacrifice of all things never has power sufficient to produce the faith necessary unto life and salvation.
15 For from the first existence of man, the faith necessary unto the enjoyment of life and salvation never could be obtained without the sacrifice of all earthly things.
16 It was through this sacrifice, and this only, that God has ordained that men should enjoy eternal life.
17 And it is through the medium of the sacrifice of all earthly things that men do actually know that they are doing the things well pleasing in the sight of God.
18 When a man has offered in sacrifice all that he has, for the truth's sake, not even withholding his life, and believes before God that he has been called to make this sacrifice because he seeks to do his will, he then knows most assuredly that God does and will accept his sacrifice and offering, and that he has not, and will not, seek his face in vain. Under these circumstances he can obtain the faith necessary to lay hold on eternal life.
19 It is vain for persons to imagine that they are heirs with those, or can be heirs with them, who have offered their all in sacrifice and thereby obtained faith in God and favor with him, unless they in like manner offer unto him the same sacrifice, and through that offering obtain the knowledge that they are accepted of him.
20 It was in offering sacrifices that Abel, the first martyr, obtained knowledge that he was accepted of God.
21 And from the days of righteous Abel to the present time, the knowledge that men have that they are accepted in the sight of God, is obtained by offering sacrifice.
22 And in the last days, before the Lord comes, he is to gather together his saints who have made a covenant with him by sacrifice: Psalms 50:3-5: Our God shall come, and shall not keep silence: a fire shall devour before him, and it shall be very tempestuous round about him. He shall call to the heavens from above, and to the earth, that he may judge his people. Gather my saints together unto me; those that have made a covenant unto me by sacrifice.
23 Those then who make this sacrifice will have the testimony that their course is pleasing in the sight of God.
24 And those who have this testimony will have faith to lay hold on eternal life, and will be enabled, through faith, to endure unto the end and receive the crown laid up for them that love the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ.
25 But those who do not make the sacrifice cannot enjoy this faith, because men are dependent upon this sacrifice in order to obtain it.
26 They cannot lay hold on eternal life because the revelations of God do not guarantee unto them authority so to do. And without this guarantee, faith cannot exist.
27 All the saints of whom we have account in all the revelations of God which are extant, obtained the knowledge they had of their acceptance in his sight through the sacrifice they offered unto him.
28 And through the knowledge thus obtained, their faith became sufficiently strong to lay hold on the promise of eternal life, and to endure "as seeing him who is invisible,” (Hebrews 11:27) and were enabled, through faith, to combat the powers of darkness, contend against the wiles of the adversary, overcome the world, and obtain the end of their faith, even the salvation of their souls.
29 But those who have not made this sacrifice to God, do not know that the course they pursue is well pleasing in his sight.
30 For whatever their belief or opinion may be, it is a matter of doubt and uncertainty in their mind. And where doubt and uncertainty are, there faith is not, nor can it be, for doubt and faith do not exist in the same person at the same time.
31 So that persons whose minds are under doubts and fears cannot have unshaken confidence.
32 And where unshaken confidence is not, there faith is weak.
33 And where faith is weak, persons will not be able to contend against all the opposition, tribulations, and afflictions which they will have to encounter in order to become heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ Jesus. And they will grow weary in their minds, and the adversary will have power of them and destroy them.

Note: This lecture is so plain, and the facts set forth so self-evident, that it is deemed unnecessary to form a catechism upon it. The student is therefore instructed to commit the whole to memory.
